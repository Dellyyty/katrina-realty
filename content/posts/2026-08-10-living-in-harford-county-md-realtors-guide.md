My office sits on Conowingo Road in Bel Air, which means Harford County isn't an area I "cover." It's where I get my coffee, sit in the Route 24 backup like everyone else, and run into past clients at the farmers market. So when buyers ask me whether they should consider Harford County, they get the local's answer, not the brochure.

Here it is.

## Who ends up in Harford County

Three groups, mostly.

**Aberdeen Proving Ground families.** APG employs tens of thousands between uniformed personnel, civilians, and contractors, and it anchors the county's east side. As a Military Relocation Professional (MRP), a big slice of my practice is PCS families landing here on short timelines. If that's you, start with my [contact page](https://kkstherealtor.com/#contact) — relocation buyers need a different playbook, and I run it constantly.

**Baltimore commuters trading up on space.** The math is straightforward: the same money that buys a rowhome in the city buys a single-family with a yard in Edgewood, Abingdon, or Aberdeen, and a genuinely nice one in Bel Air or Forest Hill. I-95 and Route 40 carry you south; MARC's Penn Line stops in Aberdeen if you'd rather read than drive.

**People leaving Baltimore County for "one more ring out."** Towson and Perry Hall families who want newer construction, larger lots, or lower price-per-square-foot keep sliding northeast up Route 1 and Route 24. It's been a steady migration pattern for a decade.

## The towns, in plain terms

**Bel Air** is the county seat and the center of gravity. Good downtown with actual restaurants, well-regarded schools, established neighborhoods from the 1970s through the 2000s plus newer townhome communities. If Harford County has a "default answer," it's Bel Air, and prices reflect that — expect the county's premium here alongside Fallston and Forest Hill.

**Havre de Grace** is the charmer. A genuine historic waterfront town where the Susquehanna meets the Chesapeake, with a promenade, a lighthouse, antique shops, and a strong sense of itself. Waterfront and water-view homes carry real premiums, but a few blocks inland the value is honestly remarkable for what you get. It has become my go-to suggestion for buyers who say they want "character."

**Aberdeen** is the practical play. Closest to APG, MARC station in town, and some of the most attainable single-family pricing in the county. It doesn't win style points against Havre de Grace, but for a first home or a rental investment near a federal employment anchor, it's hard to argue with — I touched on that investor logic in my [first-time buyer post](https://kkstherealtor.com/blog/first-time-homebuyer-maryland-what-to-know-before-touring) as well.

**Fallston, Forest Hill, Jarrettsville, and the north county** are for the space-seekers. Acre-plus lots, horse properties up toward the Pennsylvania line, and if you keep going north you're in my PA coverage area of York and Lancaster Counties anyway. Well and septic become common up here, which changes your inspection checklist — something I walk every north-county buyer through before we tour.

**Edgewood and Joppa** offer the county's most accessible entry points, with active revitalization and quick I-95 access. Block-by-block knowledge matters most here, which is exactly what a local agent is for.

## What things cost

Harford County's median sale price has generally run meaningfully below neighboring Baltimore County's premium suburbs and far below Howard or Montgomery, which is the whole arbitrage. As of this summer, county medians have hovered in the low-to-mid $400s per [Redfin's Harford County data](https://www.redfin.com/county/1290/MD/Harford-County/housing-market), with Bel Air and Fallston above that line and Aberdeen and Edgewood below it. Townhomes in the low-to-mid $300s remain findable, which in the 2026 Maryland market qualifies as good news.

Property taxes are moderate for Maryland, and the county's [government site](https://www.harfordcountymd.gov/) publishes current rates if you want to verify the line item on your estimate. I always build the real tax number into a buyer's payment math rather than the listing portal's guess.

## The honest drawbacks

Route 24 at rush hour is a lifestyle. The county's public transit outside the MARC line is thin, so plan on two cars for most households. Nightlife means Bel Air or a drive to Baltimore. And inventory here is genuinely tight — well-priced homes in Bel Air school zones still move in days, not weeks, even at today's mortgage rates.

## Is it your county?

If you want space, schools, and a real community feel within striking distance of Baltimore and APG — and you'd rather your money buy square footage than a zip code flex — Harford County belongs on your list.
