## The Data Is In: Baltimore Has a Buyer Window (And It's Coming Fast)

Redfin published [new research this week](https://www.redfin.com/news/best-time-to-buy-home-late-summer/) identifying the best time to score a deal in metros across the country. Mid-September is Baltimore's sweet spot. Not early September. Not October. The two-week stretch starting around September 10.

I've been saying some version of this to clients for years, but now we have the data. Sellers who listed in June are tired. Inventory that didn't move in July is still sitting. And buyers who waited out the summer are finally circling back, checkbooks ready but confidence high.

That last part matters.

Because while mid-September may be *the* window, buyers across Maryland, D.C., Pennsylvania, and Delaware are already acting like they own the place. [Redfin also reported](https://www.redfin.com/news/contract-cancellations-july-2026/) that **14% of U.S. homebuying deals fell through in July**—the highest share since November 2023. Fourteen percent. That means more than one in seven signed contracts died before closing.

Buyers are walking. And they're doing it with zero guilt.

## Why Buyers Have the Upper Hand (Even With Rates Still Above 6%)

Mortgage rates edged down to **6.65% this week**, according to [Freddie Mac's latest survey](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms). That's a hair lower than last week's 6.67%, but let's be honest—6.65% doesn't exactly spark a bidding war.

Still, something shifted. Inventory is up. Listings have climbed for five straight weeks, per [Redfin's August 16 data](https://www.redfin.com/news/housing-market-update-new-listings-tick-up-august/). And while that's good news for buyers in Harford, Baltimore, Anne Arundel, Howard, and Montgomery counties, it's also why cancellations are spiking.

More options mean pickier buyers. A home inspection reveals a $4,000 HVAC issue? Walk. Appraisal comes in $10K low and the seller won't budge? Walk. Another house two streets over just dropped its price? Walk.

I watched this play out last month at a townhouse in Bel Air. The buyers were pre-approved, excited, moving from Pennsylvania. Then they found out the HOA had a special assessment coming. They asked the seller to cover half. Seller said no. Deal died on a Tuesday afternoon.

Two years ago that buyer would have gritted their teeth and closed anyway.

## The Rate Story Everyone's Talking About (And Missing the Point)

There was noise this week about Treasury bond buybacks and what the government can or can't do to lower mortgage rates. [HousingWire covered it](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/what-can-the-government-do-to-lower-mortgage-rates/)—yes, the government has levers, but don't hold your breath waiting for a magic 4% rate to reappear.

Here's what I tell my first-time buyers in York, Lancaster, Prince George's, and New Castle County: stop waiting for the perfect rate. You're not buying the rate. You're buying the house. Rates will move. You can refinance. But the three-bedroom in a good school district that checks all your boxes? Someone else will buy it while you're waiting for Powell to sneeze.

The [National Association of REALTORS® reported](https://www.nar.realtor/) that pending home sales slipped 2.3% in July and were down 2.2% year-over-year. Translation: fewer people are signing contracts. Which means less competition for *you* if you're ready to move now. I wrote about the [pending sales drop earlier this week](https://kkstherealtor.com/blog/mortgage-rates-fall-pending-sales-drop-maryland-buyers-august-2026) and the takeaway hasn't changed—this is a volume problem, not a rate problem.

## Baltimore in September: What to Expect

So what does mid-September actually look like on the ground?

Expect sellers who've been on the market 60+ days to get real about pricing. Expect open houses to see thinner crowds but more serious attendees. Expect your offer to carry more weight than it did in May, even if you're asking for closing cost help.

I also expect inventory to stay elevated in Baltimore County, Harford, and Howard. We're not seeing the spring frenzy, and that's fine. What we *are* seeing is negotiation. Actual back-and-forth. Buyers who can ask for a home warranty or a radon mitigation credit without their agent laughing.

That's a functional market. And for buyers who've felt locked out for two years, it's a relief.
